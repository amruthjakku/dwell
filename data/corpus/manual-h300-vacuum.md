## 5.1 Vacuum pump overview

The chamber is evacuated by a rotary vane pump rated 40 m³/h at 50 Hz. Vacuum level is measured by sensor B21 and displayed as millibar absolute. A healthy pump reaches 8 mbar within 6 seconds on an empty chamber at ambient temperature.

Vacuum problems on this machine split into three families: a leaking chamber seal, a worn pump vane set, and a blocked exhaust filter. Confirm which family you are in before ordering parts.

## 5.2 Vacuum pump vane replacement

Vacuum vanes are a wear part. Replace the vane set every 4,000 operating hours, or sooner if the pump cannot reach 15 mbar within 10 seconds.

Required parts: [P:105330410] vane set (7 pieces) and [P:105330418] pump gasket kit. Always replace both together — a new vane set against a hardened old gasket will leak.

After replacement, run the pump for 20 minutes with the inlet open to bed in the vanes, then check the ultimate pressure again.

## 5.3 Chamber seal and door gasket

A hardened or compressed chamber gasket causes slow evacuation rather than a total failure: the machine still reaches vacuum, just too slowly. Symptom is a cycle time that creeps up over weeks and a hissing sound at the chamber edge.

Replacement part: [P:105330527] chamber gasket profile, sold per metre. The H 300 chamber requires 3.4 m.

Press a strip of paper between gasket and chamber edge and pull it out at several points — even resistance means the gasket is still serviceable.

## 5.4 Exhaust filter blockage

A blocked exhaust filter raises pump running temperature and reduces achievable vacuum. Replace the filter every 1,000 hours. Part: [P:105330633] exhaust filter element.

## 5.5 Vacuum sensor calibration

Sensor B21 drifts after roughly two years. A machine that shows good vacuum on the gauge but poor seam quality, or vice versa, usually has a drifting sensor rather than a pump fault. Calibrate against a reference gauge before replacing the pump.
