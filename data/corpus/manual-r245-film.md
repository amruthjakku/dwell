## 4.1 Film transport chain

The lower film is advanced by a chain with gripper chains on both sides. Transport faults are reported as alarms 20 to 29. Most transport faults are caused by incorrect chain tension or a worn gripper spring, not by the drive.

Chain tension is set with the tensioner on the operator side. Correct deflection is 8 mm at 50 N applied mid-span.

## 4.2 Gripper chain tension

A loose chain causes film slip, which shows up as misaligned print registration and intermittent alarm 22. A chain that is too tight causes premature bearing failure on the drive sprocket.

Gripper springs lose force after roughly 500,000 cycles. Replacement spring: [P:104220315] gripper spring, pack of 10.

## 4.3 Forming station and film heating

Forming quality depends on heater temperature, dwell and film grade. A film that webs or bridges between cavities is being formed too cold, or the heater is partially failed.

Heater zones are monitored individually. A single cold zone produces a repeatable defect in the same position across every cycle — that pattern always points to the heater, never to the film.

Replacement heater: [P:104220518] forming heater element 2.5 kW.

## 4.4 Print registration drift

Registration drift over a production run is usually a worn advance roller or a slipping encoder coupling, not a software fault. Check the encoder coupling first: a cracked coupling produces drift that increases with line speed.

## 4.5 Film edge trim and waste removal

Blocked trim extraction causes film to bunch at the chain and triggers alarm 27. Clear the extraction duct at the start of every shift on high-dust products such as flour-coated goods.
